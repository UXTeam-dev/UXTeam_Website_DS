## Using this guide for a page with no content

When a new page has no content file, build its copy from this guide in this order:

1. **Find the page's job** in [Page rules](#8-page-rules). It gives the job, what the page must include and its main action.
2. **Pick one or two pillars** from [Messaging pillars](#6-messaging-pillars) and pair each with proof from "Proof we can use today".
3. **Write the sections** in the house pattern: a two to three word eyebrow, a short heading, an optional one line subhead. Open each section with its answer.
4. **Reuse exact wording** where it exists: service labels, the boilerplate, approved lines ("Hire our judgment, not just our hands", "We use AI. We don't outsource the decision."), company facts and approved numbers.
5. **Check the voice** against [Brand voice and tone](#7-brand-voice-and-tone): say this, not that, and the vocabulary list.
6. **Check every number and claim** against [Claims and proof rules](#10-claims-and-proof-rules). Anything pending stays a placeholder.
7. **Send the draft to Chintan for sign off.** Final wording needs his approval.

**Note (repo):** hyphens in compound words are fine in website copy ("UX-led", "first-pass"). Em dashes are never used.

## 1. Purpose and how to use this guide

This guide gives designers and writers one source for UXTeam's positioning, tone, and page rules, so every page sounds and looks like the homepage.

Use it in this order:

1. Read sections 2 to 6 once: who we are, who we serve, what we sell, and why us.
2. Keep section 7 (voice) and section 8 (page rules) open while designing.
3. Check section 10 before publishing any number or claim.

The source of truth is the homepage wireframe and the decisions made while building it. Where a page and this guide disagree, flag it to Chintan instead of resolving it silently. Section 11 lists what is decided and what is pending. Anything pending is not final and must not be published.

## 2. Positioning

UXTeam is a UX-led design studio for B2B teams that wants to be hired for judgment, not only execution. We find what is holding growth back, design the fix, build it, and measure the result.

**Boilerplate** (about 40 words, for any page footer, profile, or press line):

> UXTeam is a UX-led design studio for B2B teams. We start with the metric that matters, diagnose what is holding growth back, then design, build, test, and report the result. AI speeds the work. Senior people make the call.

**Homepage headline:** We design decisions, not just screens.

**The idea in four parts:**

- **B2B first.** Our buyers are product, growth, and marketing leaders at B2B companies. Consumer and D2C brands are a supporting audience, never the lead.
- **Judgment before production.** Diagnosis and strategy come first. Design and build follow.
- **AI-enabled, human-led.** AI handles first-pass analysis, drafts, and audits at volume. People frame the problem, set priorities, and own the recommendation.
- **Outcome-focused, not outcome-guaranteed.** We agree one metric at the start and report against it. We never promise results.

**What we are not:** a pixel factory, a generic AI design agency, or a cheap build-only vendor.

## 3. Audience

Our best-fit buyer is a B2B company with real revenue at stake and no strong in-house strategist.

**Primary reader:** product, growth, and marketing leaders at B2B SaaS and AI, FinTech and insurance, real estate platforms, and event platforms, in the USA, Canada, the UAE and wider Middle East, Southeast Asia, and Australia.

**What they want from us:**

- A clear diagnosis of what is holding growth back.
- A senior person who understands the problem.
- A roadmap their team can act on.
- A measurable result, reported honestly.

**Secondary audiences:**

- **Agencies that want a white label partner.** They have their own pages and a more practical tone: confidential, dependable, strategy plus capacity.
- **Select consumer and D2C brands.** They get one supporting page and an FAQ answer, never homepage space.

**Not a fit:** teams with a strong internal UX and strategy function who only want extra hands; build-only buyers shopping on price; single deliverables with no goal behind them.

**Design implication:** write every direct-client page for one reader, a B2B leader deciding whether to trust us with a business problem. Keep agency-to-agency language off those pages.

## 4. Offer

We sell seven services through three ways of working, and the way of working changes by service.

**Services** (labels are final; use them exactly):

| Service | What it does for the client | Usual way to work |
|---|---|---|
| UX audit | Finds what is costing pipeline and activation before a redesign | Standalone paid audit (UXAudit), or a small audit that opens a project |
| UX strategy workshop | A working session that ends in a prioritized roadmap tied to growth metrics, scoped to the client's need and the project | Scoped per client, often alongside a Project |
| Product and app design | Onboarding, dashboards, and workflows users adopt | Project (opens with a small audit) or Partnership |
| Web and marketing design | Websites that turn demand into demos and trials, built in Webflow or WordPress | Project (opens with a small audit) |
| Conversion optimization | Demo, trial, and sign-up funnels, tested and measured | Partnership |
| Design systems | One system for every team shipping the product | Project (opens with a small audit) |
| AI design for B2B products | AI agent experience, AI interface design, trust and transparency | Project (opens with a small audit) |

The UX strategy workshop has no fixed format. It is scoped to the client's need and the nature of the project, so pages describe it only as "a working session that ends in a prioritized roadmap" and never state a length or price.

**Three ways to work, in the order a buyer usually moves:**

- **Audit: clarity before you commit.** Best for teams unsure what is holding growth back. Typical turnaround: a report in about 3 days after we get access to your analytics. Priced as a fixed fee, quoted after one call. It is the paid, standalone audit (UXAudit). Every project also opens with a small audit of the product or app, and that audit is part of the project. It is also the entry point, so it carries "Start here".
- **Project: a defined piece of work.** Best for a clear goal and scope, such as a redesign, a launch, or a build. Length varies by scope, and no timeline is published. Every project opens with a small audit, as part of the project.
- **Partnership: ongoing design, backed by strategy.** Best for teams that need senior design and testing every month. The minimum term depends on the service and is agreed on the first call (per-service terms are pending).

**Section line used on the homepage:** Hire our judgment, not just our hands.

White label is a secondary channel for agencies. It never leads the homepage, and it keeps its own pages and tone.

## 5. Point of view

Production is getting cheaper, and judgment is not. That is the belief behind everything on the site.

**What we believe:**

1. **Start with the outcome, not the screen.** We agree the business metric before anyone opens a design file.
2. **Diagnose before you design.** The audit comes first, so decisions rest on evidence, not taste.
3. **Design and build together.** One team means nothing is lost between design and code.
4. **Test what ships.** Changes are measured against the agreed metric and reported honestly.
5. **Senior people lead.** The client gets experienced judgment from day one, not a learning curve.

**How we talk about AI:** openly and without hype. We use AI where it speeds the work (first-pass analysis, drafts, variations, audits at volume). We do not claim it replaces judgment, and we do not position against it. Line to reuse: **We use AI. We don't outsource the decision.**

**How we talk about execution:** we sell it, and we are good at it. The point is that execution alone is not what clients are hiring us for. Use contrast lines such as "not just screens" and "not just our hands" sparingly, no more than two on a page.

## 7. Brand voice and tone

UXTeam sounds like a senior colleague who tells you the truth plainly: warm, personal, specific, and sure of its judgment without being loud. This tone is now approved: warmer and more personal. Final wording needs Chintan's sign-off.

**Six principles:**

1. **Plain and conversational.** Write the way a senior colleague talks across the table: short sentences, common words, and contractions like we're, you'll, and don't.
2. **Confident, not loud.** Say what we do and what we have measured. Never announce how good we are.
3. **Specific.** Numbers, names, and examples beat adjectives.
4. **Personal.** Speak to one reader as you, and name their situation before offering the fix, for example: *You've got traffic. Let's find out why it isn't converting.* "We" for the team. "I" only where the founder speaks directly, such as the booking section.
5. **Honest about limits.** Say what we do not do and what we do not promise.
6. **Warm and professional.** Friendly and kind, never slang, never hype. No exclamation marks.

**Say this, not that:**

| Say this | Not this |
|---|---|
| We find what is holding growth back. | We unlock explosive growth with cutting-edge AI. |
| We agree one metric and report against it. | We guarantee results. |
| Senior experts lead the work. | A world-class team of rockstar designers. |
| We use AI. We don't outsource the decision. | AI-powered everything. |
| Hire our judgment, not just our hands. | We are not like other agencies. |

**Vocabulary.**
- **Prefer:** diagnose, decision, metric, outcome, judgment, senior, build, test, report, partner.
- **Avoid:** unlock, leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class, disrupt, revolutionize, seamless, solutions, pixel-perfect.

**House style:**

- Sentence case for all headings and buttons, except the primary button, which is "Book a Discovery Call".
- Section pattern: a two-to-three word eyebrow, a short heading, an optional one-line subhead.
- Headings are short and often in the first person ("Outcomes we've delivered", "We use AI. We don't outsource the decision.").
- No em dashes anywhere in copy.
- American spelling.
- Calls to action: "Book a Discovery Call" is the main one, and "Start with an audit" belongs to the audit. Never "Contact us" or "Get a quote".

## 8. Page rules

Every page has one job and one main action, and the homepage sets the pattern the others follow.

| Page | Its job | Must include | Main action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Earn trust fast and route each visitor | Hero, outcome case studies, success stories, Why UXTeam, services, how we work, ways to work, industries, FAQ, booking | Book a Discovery Call |
| UXAudit | Make the audit tangible and low-risk | What is inside the report, a preview of a sample report, turnaround, what we need, price or "quoted after one call" | Start with an audit |
| What we do | Explain each service in plain terms | One section per service with an anchor, a short FAQ for each, how it works as a project or partnership | Book a Discovery Call |
| Conversion optimization | Show the testing method and approved results | Method, funnel analysis example (illustrative, no client data), VORO and AgentSelect results, request for CRO case studies under NDA | Book a Discovery Call |
| AI agent experience | Define the offer and prove it | A plain definition, from-to table limited to rows we can back, the AI interface case study, FAQ | Book a Discovery Call |
| How we work | Show the process and the engagement terms | Story timeline, then length, what we need, minimum term, reporting, pricing notes, NDA and IP | Book a Discovery Call |
| White label | Win agencies as partners | Why agencies choose us, ways to partner, partnership stories, comparison table (neutral wording), agency FAQ | Book a Discovery Call |
| Industries | Show relevant depth per industry | One line per industry, relevant problems, approved proof only | Book a Discovery Call |
| Case studies | Prove outcomes and judgment | Outcome case studies first, project case studies, success stories filter, problem then decision then result | Book a Discovery Call |
| Insights | Show how we think | Dated posts, topic labels, the funnel analysis template | Book a Discovery Call |
| About | Make the company real | Plain definition in the first paragraph, story, team with names and photos, locations | Book a Discovery Call |
| Contact | Make booking effortless | Three-field form (name, work email, company website), then a calendar on the same page, who they will speak with | Book a Discovery Call |

**Why choose UXTeam** is its own page. Its job is to give direct clients the three pillars in depth, with proof and a short FAQ. Its main action is Book a Discovery Call.

**Rules that apply to every page:**

- **Open with the point.** Each page and each section starts with its answer.
- **One main call to action per screen.** Secondary links are text links.
- **Proof near claims.** A claim without a number, a name, or a quote gets one or gets cut.
- Keep long details (prices, terms, NDA) on How we work, and link to them from other pages.
- Answers and definitions must be real text on the page, not baked into images or hidden behind tabs, so search and AI engines can read them.
- Repeated answers (FAQ, numbers, terms) must match across pages.

## 9. Design and content principles

The look is restrained and refined: it should feel senior, calm, and exact. Colors, type sizes, and spacing are not defined yet (see Pending decisions). Until they are, follow the live homepage and ask Chintan before introducing any new style.

**Note (repo):** colors, type and spacing are now defined in the Figma Design System Guideline (2351:577) and coded in this repo. See [DESIGN-RULES.md](DESIGN-RULES.md).

**Principles decided so far:**

- **Restraint.** Hairline rules, generous whitespace, one accent color, no clip-art, and no stock icons.
- **Type.** Headings in medium weight, body in regular, sentence case throughout. Follow the homepage pairing for eyebrows and story lines.
- **Icons.** Outline style on a 24px grid, 1.5px stroke, using the current text color. The process icon set is provided.
- **Imagery.** Real, client-approved work beats stock. Treat each set the same way (one crop, one corner radius, one overlay). The founder photo appears only in the booking section. The team page uses real photos with names.
- **Proof first.** Numbers lead on outcome cards. Always show what the number measures and the test length.
- **Honest illustrations.** Anything illustrative is labeled "Illustrative" and uses no client data.

**Layout patterns already decided on the homepage** (reuse them on inner pages):

- **Outcome case studies:** dark cards with the result number large, client name above, test length beneath.
- **Services:** grouped under buyer questions ("Not sure what's wrong?", "Ready to build?", "Want it to perform and scale?"), one row per service.
- **Process:** a hairline timeline with five steps, each with an icon, a story line, and a detail line.
- **Ways to work:** a ladder that starts with the audit.
- **FAQ:** two categories (Our approach, Working together), answer-first.
- **Booking:** a three-field form, then a calendar, with the founder's name and a short promise.

**Accessibility and build:**

- Text contrast of at least 4.5 to 1, including gray body text and captions.
- Respect reduced-motion settings. Hover effects need a tap or keyboard equivalent.
- On mobile, timelines stack vertically and tables scroll inside their own container.
- Every answer, definition, and number lives in the page's HTML text.

## 10. Claims and proof rules

No claim goes live unless it is true on every project and we can show how we know.

**Rules:**

1. **No guarantees.** We agree a metric and report against it. Never promise a result, and never use "guaranteed" or "outcome-based pricing".
2. Every number names what it measures, the baseline, and the period. Show absolute rates beside any relative lift.
3. Client names, logos, screens, and figures need written approval. If a client is under NDA, anonymize by sector and scale.
4. Placeholders such as [X] and [confirm] never go live.
5. **One source for each number.** The same figure and wording appear on the homepage, About page, LinkedIn, and decks.
6. "Senior" and "led by" claims must be true for every engagement.
7. Do not publish an audit of a named company that did not ask for one.
8. Label anything illustrative as "Illustrative", with no client data.
9. Agency partner results stay private. White label work is confidential by default.
